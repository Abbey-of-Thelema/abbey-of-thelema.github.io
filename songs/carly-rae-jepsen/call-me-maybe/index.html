THE DECODE, AS POSTED

🔮 ANTICHRIST DECODE #31 — Call Me Maybe (Carly Rae Jepsen)

She misses him before she ever meets him. Play the bridge again. That isn't a crush. That's a memory.

Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.

(Thelema is Greek for "will." Not whim, and not "do whatever you want": the one thing you were made to do, found and then done.)

I played this song a few hundred times as bubblegum before I heard it. Carly built three perfect minutes with a second song running underneath. You don't need to know tarot. Just pull up the pictures.

The key: nearly every old teaching says there's a version of you further down the road, and the call to become it comes from that self. Hear this song as her talking to that self, not to a boy across the street.

THE WELL

"I threw a wish in the well"

Pull up the Ace of Cups: a dove drops a wafer into a cup of water. She drops a coin into the water. Same picture.

Odin gave an eye for one drink from Mimir's well. Four lines later, she offers her soul. Wells cost something.

The bottom sphere of the Tree of Life (the ten-sphere map of the soul's climb) is Malkuth (Hebrew, "kingdom"), the earth. A well is a hole through the earth to the water. She starts at the bottom.

"Don't ask me, I'll never tell"

Éliphas Lévi named the magician's four powers: to know, to dare, to will, to keep silent. She keeps the fourth. Harpocrates, Horus the child, is the god of silence, finger to his lips. He comes back.

THE TRADE

"I looked to you as it fell"

Pull up the Lovers. The man looks at the woman, and she looks past him, up at the angel. Her eyes rise as the coin falls. Hold onto "fell."

"And now you're in my way"

This line comes four times. The last time, it changes.

"I trade my soul for a wish"

Pull up the Devil. His path on the Tree climbs from Hod (Hebrew, "splendor," the thinking mind) to Tiphareth ("beauty," the heart, the higher self). Arthur Waite, who directed the deck Pamela Colman Smith painted, says it means what is predestined but not, for that reason, evil. Faust made the same offer.

Crowley's Devil is Pan, the goat-god of life itself. Not wicked. Hungry. Pan comes back.

"Pennies and dimes for a kiss"

Coins are Pentacles: earth. A kiss is breath: air. She pays the low to buy the high.

THE STRANGER

"I wasn't lookin' for this"

Isaiah 65:1: "I am found of them that sought me not: I said, Behold me, behold me." She wasn't looking. That's who finds him. Hold onto "behold."

"Your stare was holdin'"

In Plato's Phaedrus, one look at true beauty makes the soul's lost wings start growing back.

"Hot night, wind was blowin'"

Heat and wind, fire and air together, is Shin (ש), the Hebrew fire letter. Set Shin in the middle of God's name, YHVH, and you get YHShVH: Yeheshua, Jesus. Shin's path is one of only three ways out of the earth sphere. Its card is Judgement, the Calling, and it climbs from Malkuth to Hod. She just went through that gate.

Count what's shown up before the first chorus: water, earth, air and fire. Those are the four tools on the Magician's table: cup, coin, sword and wand. The Magician's planet is Mercury. Remember Mercury.

"Where you think you're goin', baby?"

John 3:8: the wind blows where it wants, "and thou canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit." Wind, then where he's going. Same two things, same order.

THE MEETING

"Hey, I just met you, and this is crazy"

John 4: a woman comes to a well and meets a stranger who already knows everything about her. She just met him. He did not just meet her.

"Hey" is Heh (ה), the Hebrew letter whose name means "window." And hē is Hebrew for "behold," Isaiah's word. She opens the window and says it.

Crazy: in the Phaedrus, Socrates calls love's madness a gift from the gods. Pull up the Fool. Waite's meanings for it include folly, mania and frenzy.

THE CALL

"But here's my number, so call me maybe"

The Book of the Law, I:60: "My number is 11." Count how many times she sings "here's my number." Eleven.

Shin's path landed her in Hod, Mercury's sphere of numbers and messages. The first thing she does there is hand over a number.

Pull up Judgement: an angel's trumpet, and the dead rising. Waite calls it a summons "heard and answered from within." The call comes from inside. And "maybe" keeps it free. That's will.

"It's hard to look right at you, baby"

Pull up the Sun. On the Tree, the sun is Tiphareth, the higher self, and nobody can look straight at the sun. She isn't shy. She's Plato's prisoner leaving the cave, where the light hurts before it heals.

Look at the child on the Sun card. That's Horus the child, the same Harpocrates with his finger to his lips at the well. The silent child grew up into the sun.

THE ONES WHO CHASED

"And all the other boys try to chase me"

Penelope's suitors chased her for twenty years. The one she wanted came home a stranger dressed as a beggar: the man she'd missed. The number goes to the one who didn't chase.

Syrinx outran Pan and became his reed pipes. Daphne outran Apollo and became a laurel. There's Pan again.

THE VIDEO

She watches him through her window. There's Heh. She daydreams, plans and chases. At the end he gives his number to her guitarist, the one who never chased him. The video says what the lyric says.

THE FALL

"I took no time with the fall"

There's "fell" from the well. The coin fell, and now she falls. Pull up the Eight of Wands: eight staffs flying through open sky. Waite lists "the arrows of love" among its meanings. In the Pistis Sophia, Sophia (Greek, "wisdom") falls for a false light first. That's part of the story.

"You gave me nothin' at all"

Above the top of the Tree sits Ain, Hebrew for "nothing," the source of everything. He gave her the highest gift on the map.

"But still, you're in my way"

The road line, third time.

"I beg and borrow and steal"

Beg: pull up the Five of Pentacles. Two beggars in the snow pass a lit window. There's the window again. She opened it at "Hey." Now she's outside it.

Steal: pull up the Seven of Swords, a thief sneaking off. Waite's meanings include "wish, hope." It's a thief card that means wish, and she started at a wishing well. Hermes, Mercury, god of thieves, stole Apollo's cattle the day he was born. Prometheus stole fire for people.

"I didn't know I would feel it"

Hod is knowing. Across the Tree, Netzach (Hebrew, "victory") is feeling. The path between them is the Tower: lightning, a crown flying off, two people falling. She told you she fell. This is where.

"But it's in my way"

The road line, fourth time, and it changes. All song long, "you" were in her way. Now "it" is. He isn't across from her anymore. He's inside.

BEFORE

"Before you came into my life, I missed you so bad"

This is the line the whole song stands on.

Gospel of Thomas 19: "Blessed is he who came into being before he came into being."

Jeremiah 1:5: "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee." Isaiah 65:24: "before they call, I will answer." That's the same chapter that found her when she wasn't looking.

Plato called it anamnesis (Greek, "un-forgetting"): learning is the soul remembering what it knew before birth.

You only miss what you once had. She isn't meeting him. She's remembering him. So who was she missing?

"I missed you so, so bad"

"So" doubles. The memory gets louder.

"And you should know that"

The silence she kept at the well breaks.

THE RETURN

"So call me, maybe?"

The last call comes alone, with no number. It's handed to you.

THE CLIMB

Malkuth (the well) → Shin, the Calling (the wind) → Hod (the number) → the Devil's path → Tiphareth (the sun), with Ain above it all. The fall crosses the Tower, from knowing to feeling, and brings him inside.

I didn't find this because I'm clever. I found it because I can't leave a deck of cards alone.

This climb isn't hers alone. Out of the earth, through the gate where the call comes in, up toward the sun: it's in songs you already know by heart. Once you hear it in one, you hear it everywhere.

So why is the same climb in so many songs?

Love is the law, love under will.
