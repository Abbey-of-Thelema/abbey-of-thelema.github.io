THE DECODE, AS POSTED

Love Like Winter — AFI, Decemberunderground, 2006

Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.

Thelema is Greek for Will. Not do whatever you want, not whim. The will that is actually yours, as against appetite. I gloss it every time because most people who have heard the sentence have never been told what the word in it means.

Not because of anything I found. Because of something you are about to notice yourself, and then cannot stop noticing. I did this to myself first and I have not been able to hear the song since.

Whatever this band thought they were doing, they built a love song with more architecture in it than a love song needs. That is the part worth admiring, and it is the reason I have spent longer on four minutes of music than any reasonable person would.

I keep ending up in the same part of the same map with songs that have nothing to do with each other. Either the map is real or I am very consistent.

Route: Malkuth → Path 32 (Tav) → Yesod → Path 25 (Samekh) → Tiphereth → Return to Malkuth

MALKUTH — the Kingdom

The body, and the recording.

He says that he is made of sugar and ice, and then says it again.

PROFANE. A cold man being poetic about being cold.

INITIATE. He does not say I am cold. He says made — passive, done to him by something else — and repeats it, the way you repeat yourself when you can tell nobody caught it.

Two opposites named as the material of one man. The body is declared before anything happens to it, and it is declared unstable.

That is the whole song stated in the first verse, and I walked past it for a long time.

PATH 32 (TAV) — the Universe

The door out.

Here, it is December every day. And in the opening line he warns his own warmth to turn away.

PROFANE. It is always winter for him. Sad song, sad man.

INITIATE. Not a season. An address.

Winter is the one condition that both destroys a body and preserves it — the same ice does both, and a sculpture is what is left when they happen at once.

And the very first line has him addressing his warmth as a second person standing in the room. Line one, two people, one man.

Press your lips to the sculptures, and surely you will stay.

PROFANE. Kiss the art. Stay in the romance.

INITIATE. He does not tell you to kiss a woman. He tells you to kiss the frozen form.

That is how you remain in December. You stay by fastening your mouth to the image. The door of the World card is a surface. Nothing here is the thing itself.

YESOD — the Foundation

The image and the reflection.

She exhales vanilla lace. He barely dreamt her yesterday. Lines are read in a mirror through a lipstick trace. Por siempre — forever, in a language that is not his. Then a woman tells a man he is somewhere far away, to his face.

PROFANE. A dream about a girl who wears perfume and is emotionally distant.

INITIATE. Nothing at this station is the thing itself.

Everything is seen through something else: a breath, a dream, a mirror, a trace, somebody else’s language. Vanilla lace is the veil. It is exhaled, which means it is already leaving. The Foundation doing what the Foundation does.

Then the grammar breaks. He has been saying I for the entire verse, and the last four words say his. The speaker leaves his own sentence and stands across the room watching himself be spoken to.

To his face is an English idiom for telling a man something honestly while he stands in front of you. Here it is the proof that he is not there. The phrase means the opposite of what the phrase means, and the band put it at the end of the verse where you would not stop on it.

The Abramelin says the Guardian Angel is already about you, though invisible. That is the same sentence as far away, to his face. Present. Not available as a body.

PATH 25 (SAMEKH) — the Arrow

Temperance.

The chorus, three times. He bit my lip. Then she bit my lip. Then he again. Same mouth, same bite, same from years before.

PROFANE. Androgyny. Goth band, blurred gender, we get it.

INITIATE. A person standing in a room forces a song to pick a body. This one will not, three separate times, and it is on the recording.

You do not need me for that part. Play it.

Before the bite, the chorus says two other sentences the romance reading cannot carry.

It’s in the blood. It’s in the blood.

PROFANE. Passion. Family. Something inherited and sticky.

INITIATE. Crowley writes it in Magick in Theory and Practice without metaphor: the blood is the life.

The chorus does not open with a lover. It opens with the current. Love, in that book, is the cup. The song puts the current first and the face second.

He wanted love. I taste of blood.

PROFANE. A man wanted affection. The singer is doing something with blood. The two clauses do not follow.

INITIATE. That is the leap. Want is the lower height asking. Taste is the one who already met this before birth.

Same man. Two attached. The victory rests with him.

Then the bite, and: drank my warmth.

PROFANE. Vampire image. Sexy and cold.

INITIATE. Verse one told warmth to turn away. The chorus drinks it from years before.

You were warned off the substance that is then taken. Two times. One current. That is what temperare means.

Row 25 of 777 is Temperance.

Everyone reads that card as restraint — moderation, patience, hold back. Wrong word. It is Latin temperare, to mix. It is the word for tempering steel, and it is where temperament comes from.

The card shows one figure, two cups, and liquid crossing between them. It never meant holding back. It means two substances becoming one workable body.

Then look along the same row, in the column of decan images — the astrological figures filed on that row, not a description of the card itself. Printed in the book:

A man with 3 bodies, 1 black, 1 red, 1 white.

One man. Three bodies. He. She. He.

I have owned that book for years. I found that row the other week. That should tell you how carefully I have been reading it.

Crowley renamed Temperance Art so you would stop hearing hold back. One body. Two heads. The colours swapped from the card that still showed two people. The recording already performed the counterchange.

The letter on that row is Samekh, and its path runs from the Foundation to the centre — Crowley calls it the threshold.

Which is why the operation for reaching the Angel is titled Liber Samekh, named for the road it walks. Its subtitle is Congressus Cum Daemone. Congress with the Demon.

The ritual for meeting your Angel is titled for meeting your Demon.

Mathers says the same thing in his introduction to the Abramelin: man is the middle nature between the Angels and the Demons, and to each man is attached naturally both a Guardian Angel and a Malevolent Demon, so that it rests with him to give the victory to whichever he will.

Two attached. A third standing between them who decides. That third one is the body that is neither black nor white.

TIPHERETH — Beauty

The centre.

I met my love before I was born.

PROFANE. A romantic line about soulmates.

INITIATE. Do the subtraction. No fixed gender. No fixed body. Present before his birth.

Between them, those three facts eliminate every human being who has ever lived, and somebody is still in the song.

What is left is the part of you that was there before you had a personality to defend. Crowley’s own commentary calls the Holy Guardian Angel the Unconscious Creature Self. Not a visitor. The oldest resident.

He also says elsewhere that he could have called it God, or the Higher Self, or Adi-Buddha, or sixty-one other things, having found that they were all one, and that he chose the Angel wording precisely because it sounds absurd enough that nobody would waste time defending it as a theory.

And for anyone about to say androgyny and stop: he names Aiwass as his own Holy Guardian Angel and Satan-Hadit as the Devil of his own unit of the universe — not Man’s enemy, but the one who said know thyself and taught initiation, whose emblem is Baphomet, the Androgyne.

The objection is the Demon’s own emblem, in the paragraph where he names his Angel.

RETURN TO MALKUTH

Love like winter. Then: three, four. Then the first chorus again.

PROFANE. A hook. A count-in. A studio thing. Sounds cool.

INITIATE. The title returns as the condition you never left. December is still the address.

A count-in is four beats. You were given the last two. Where are one and two?

Three is Binah, and Binah is Saturn — the beginning and the end, the gate the whole thing returns through. Four is Chesed, and the one filed there is the child who was hidden and came back.

This song counts you in from the middle and prints the beginning at the end. Everything that mattered to it happened in a stretch it refuses to narrate, and it tells you so more than once — a meeting placed before his birth, a dream he can barely account for, years named only as before.

The last chorus is the first chorus with somebody standing in it.

In Crowley’s Wake World the child climbs the whole palace and is still Lola. The lover only changes the surname. Daydream becomes Wide-awake. Always Lola, because she is the Key of Delights. The body never leaves the first house.

That is why the same words can be sung twice and mean more the second time. Illumination descends into the recording that never left Malkuth.

I am not smarter than anybody reading this. Both books have been in print for over a century and I opened them. I have been at this sixteen years and I still cannot tell you the answer.

That is not modesty. Crowley writes that no rules can be laid down for this, because it is the particular secret of each one of us — a secret not to be told or even divined by any other, whatever his grade — and that the Masters of his own Order therefore instituted no ritual for the central work of the Order.

So I am not going to tell you what he met before he was born. The man whose table I have been quoting the whole way down says that nobody can.

And none of this requires the band to have known any of it. I am not claiming they did, and I would not want them to. The claim is smaller and stranger: a man writing honestly about something he cannot name tends to land in a drawer that was already labelled.

If the mouth never changes and the face never stays, which of the three bodies was he kissing?

Love is the law, love under will.

666 — Antichrist

If this landed, there are more.

Same route, different catalogs. Oops!… I Did It Again runs the full climb and returns to the same chorus it started on. Cold as Ice runs the bottom half and freezes before the centre — the door of the World card shut from the inside. The Fate of Ophelia is a list of cards you can check with nothing but the pictures.

None of them were built to fit each other. That is the part I would check first if I were you.

Then take a song you actually love and ask the only question that matters: what if the you is not someone else? Run it yourself. See what opens.

Notes

Liber 777 Revised — Aleister Crowley, 1909. Public domain. Row 25 is Samekh, Sagittarius, Temperance. The three-bodies figure is in the column of Magical Images of the Decans (Ascendant), on that row. In the Magical Powers column, row 10 is the Vision of the Holy Guardian Angel or of Adonai; row 6 is the Vision of the Harmony of Things and the Mysteries of the Crucifixion. The tables are read across; any row can be checked in a minute.

The Sacred Magic of Abramelin the Mage — S. L. MacGregor Mathers, 1897. Public domain. The Angel-and-Demon passage is in the translator’s introduction, where Mathers summarises the system, not in Abraham’s own text to his son. The book never says the Angel precedes birth; it says the Angel is already about you, though invisible.

Magick in Theory and Practice — 1929, Part III of Book 4. “The blood is the life” is in the chapters on sacrifice and the Eucharist. The Unconscious Creature Self line is in the commentary to Liber Samekh. The appendix number varies between editions; the title does not. Subtitle as printed: Congressus Cum Daemone.

The Book of Thoth — Art / ATU XIV. Crowley renames Temperance Art. The two heads counterchange. The recording is the witness.

Konx Om Pax — Aleister Crowley, 1907. Public domain. “The Wake World”: Lola Daydream becomes Lola Wide-awake. Always Lola.

Love Like Winter — AFI, Decemberunderground, 2006. Everything above the first table reference is on the recording. Do not take my word for any of it.
