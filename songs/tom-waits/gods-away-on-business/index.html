THE DECODE, AS POSTED

🔮 God's Away on Business (Tom Waits) — ANTICHRIST DECODE #34

This song plays over the end credits of the Enron movie. Its chorus is an alibi. Its first line is the motive.

Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.

(Thelema is Greek for "will." It doesn't mean whatever you can get away with while the boss is out.)

Alibi is Latin. It means "somewhere else." That's the whole chorus: he was somewhere else. The Bible has heard this one before. In Ezekiel the land is "full of blood," and the people who filled it have their line ready: "The LORD hath forsaken the earth." Remember who was saying it.

I'm not telling you what Tom Waits and Kathleen Brennan meant. They wrote it for a stage production of Woyzeck, and the man who made the Enron film says they were the ones who suggested it for his credits. What they built does a whole sermon's work in under three minutes, from the wrong pulpit, and you can stomp to it. None of what follows needs them to have known any of it. I'm laying old maps over the lines and showing you where they touch.

One man sings this. Listen to the pronouns. There's an "I" with a price list and a "you" with a heart. Hear them as one person at two heights: the part that's selling and the part that's for sale. S. L. MacGregor Mathers, introducing the Book of Abramelin (an old manual for meeting your own angel), says every man has attached to him "both a Guardian Angel and a Malevolent Demon," and that "with him it rests to give the victory unto the which he will." This song is the second one talking.

FOUR CARDS FIRST

You can check these four with an image search and no training.

"Digging up the dead with a shovel and a pick"

Somebody is being paid to turn graves out. Now find the tarot card called Judgement.

The dead are getting up out of their coffins. It's the same picture, except that on the card an angel is calling them with a trumpet. The song left the angel out.

The chorus says God has left the building. The sixteenth tarot trump (the trumps are the twenty-two picture cards) is the Tower. Aleister Crowley's tables list it under an older name: "The House of God." A. E. Waite, whose deck with the artist Pamela Colman Smith is the one most people know, records another old name for the same card: "Castle of Plutus." Plutus is the Greek god of money.

One card. God's house and the money god's castle. The picture is lightning hitting it and the tenants falling out.

"The poor, the lame, the blind"

Three kinds of people get left on the ship. Find the Five of Pentacles, the picture at the top of this page. Two beggars in the snow, one on crutches, passing under a lit church window. The lights are on in God's house. Nobody comes to the door.

"If you're looking for someone to pull you out of that ditch"
"You're out of luck, you're out of luck"

Nobody is going to give you a hand, and your luck has run out. Find the Wheel of Fortune.

Waite's list of what this card means includes the word "luck." Every trump also carries a Hebrew letter. This one's is Kaph, and Kaph means the palm of a hand. A hand, then luck. He said one card twice.

That's four cards out of one song, and all I did was point.

THE MAP

The Tree of Life is a diagram: ten spheres joined by twenty-two paths, with a tarot trump on every path. People have read it for centuries as the way a soul climbs. This song runs it the other way.

The heart. Down the Devil's path to business. Down Judgement's path to the ground.

THE PRICE

"I'd sell your heart to the junkman"
"Baby, for a buck, for a buck"

Tiphareth (Hebrew, "beauty") is the sphere in the middle of the Tree, and it is the heart. Hod (Hebrew, "splendor") belongs to Mercury, the god of traders. Exactly one path joins those two, and its card is the Devil. So the first thing the song does is walk the Devil's path downhill: a heart, into trade.

The deck has a heart card. The Three of Swords is a heart with three blades through it, and Waite's meanings for it begin "Removal, absence."

The Sermon on the Mount: "For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also." (Matthew 6:21)

Crowley's Book of the Law, which he said was dictated to him in Cairo in 1904, has its god say where he lives: "I am the flame that burns in every heart of man, and in the core of every star." (chapter two, verse 6)

Put those side by side. The thing that just got a price tag is the place that book says God lives. That's line one. The chorus hasn't started yet.

THE DITCH

"If you're looking for someone to pull you out of that ditch"
"You're out of luck, you're out of luck"

Malkuth (Hebrew, "kingdom") is the bottom sphere, the ground. That's where the "you" is, or just under it. You've seen the card: no hand, no luck. The Wheel's planet is Jupiter, and Jupiter's sphere on the Tree is Chesed, which is Hebrew for "mercy." In two lines the hand, the luck and the mercy are all taken away.

The Gospel of Thomas is a collection of Jesus's sayings that sat buried in Egypt until 1945. One of them: "If you bring forth what is within you, what you bring forth will save you." It goes on to say what happens if you don't. That's the same news the song gives. Nobody else is coming. He sneers it. Thomas teaches it.

THE SHIP

"Ship is sinking / The ship is sinking / The ship is sinking / There's leak, there's a leak, in the boiler room"

Now it's a ship, it's going down, and the trouble is below, in the boiler room.

Crowley's 777 is his big table of what goes with what. Its note for the letter of the Chariot card reads: "The Chariot containing Life. The Secret of the Universe. Ark." An ark. And the tool it lists for the same card is "The Furnace." A ship with a furnace in it.

The water has a card too. The Hanged Man's letter, Mem, means water, and 777's note for it reads: "The Man drowned in the 'womb' flood." When a lyric sounds like drowning, read the Hanged Man first. It's the part of you that has to go under. Nobody is being told to hurt themselves.

Both of those paths are on the left side of the Tree. Hold that.

The Bible has one sinking ship that is also a business. Ezekiel 27 sends "thy merchandise, thy mariners, and thy pilots, thy calkers" to the bottom "in the day of thy ruin." Calkers are the crew who plug leaks. And the owner, one chapter on: "I am a God, I sit in the seat of God, in the midst of the seas."

THE GUEST LIST

"The poor, the lame, the blind"

You've seen the card. Here's the list. In Luke 14 a man gives a great supper, and the invited guests all send excuses. One has bought land. One has bought oxen. It's all business. So the host sends for other guests: "call the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind." Thomas tells the same story and ends it flat: "Businessmen and merchants will not enter the Places of My Father."

The song has that guest list, in that order. It only changed who's away on business.

THE CREW

"Who are the ones that we kept in charge?"
"Killers, thieves, and lawyers"

The song asks one question in three minutes and answers it in the next breath. The question says "we."

Take the answer to the Tree. Killers belong to Mars, and Mars's sphere is Geburah (Hebrew, "strength"; the old books also call it Severity). Thieves and lawyers belong to Mercury, the god of traders, thieves and talkers, and Mercury's sphere is Hod, where the heart got sold to.

Those two spheres stand on the same side of the Tree: the left-hand pillar, the Pillar of Severity. There's a third sphere on that pillar, at the top. It's Binah, Saturn's sphere, and 777's note on Saturn calls it "the hidden God." The two paths that join those three spheres are the two you just walked: the Ark with its Furnace, and the drowned man in the flood. A hidden God, a ship with a boiler room, killers, drowning, thieves and lawyers. He has been reading you one side of the Tree.

The other side is the Pillar of Mercy. You've met it once so far. It was the luck that ran out.

777 also lists what moves into each sphere when the light goes out. It calls them the Qliphoth (Hebrew, "shells"). In Mercy's sphere: "Smiters." In the heart's: "The Litigation." In Hod's: "The False Accuser."

And the "we." In 1 Samuel 8 the people demand a king, and they're warned what comes after: "ye shall cry out in that day because of your king which ye shall have chosen you; and the LORD will not hear you in that day."

THE ALIBI

"God's away, God's away / God's away on business / Business / God's away, God's away / God's away on business / Business"

Somewhere else. You've met Ezekiel's version. Here are three more.

Matthew 24. A master goes away and leaves a servant in charge. "But and if that evil servant shall say in his heart, My lord delayeth his coming; And shall begin to smite his fellowservants." The man left in charge says the boss is away, and starts hitting.

Proverbs 7. A woman is drawing a young man in, and this is her pitch: "For the goodman is not at home, he is gone a long journey: He hath taken a bag of money with him." He's away. On business. With the money.

1 Kings 18. The prophet Elijah watches the priests of a rival god, Baal, pray all morning and get nothing, and he mocks them: "either he is talking, or he is pursuing, or he is in a journey, or peradventure he sleepeth." It's the Bible's one joke about a god who's away on business, and it's told about somebody else's god.

So in that book this sentence belongs to the people who filled the land with blood, the servant who beats the staff, and the woman with the trap. The one time a prophet says it, he's laughing.

Don't the faithful say it too? They do. Psalm 10 opens: "Why standest thou afar off, O LORD?" But that's said to him. Ten verses later the same psalm gives the other version to the wicked: "He hath said in his heart, God hath forgotten: he hideth his face; he will never see it." One psalm, two voices, two heights.

Now the Tree. Kether (Hebrew, "crown") is the top sphere, and 777 lists its old titles. Two of them: "Concealed of the Concealed." "The Head which is Not." The top of the Tree was always called hidden. Hidden isn't gone.

And the Book of the Law has its god say the chorus himself: "I am alone: there is no God where I am." (chapter two, verse 23) It's the same sentence, and he isn't complaining.

THE GRAVEDIGGER

"Digging up the dead with a shovel and a pick"
"It's a job, it's a job"

Verse two has no "I," no "you" and no "we." Things just happen.

You've seen Judgement with the angel missing. 777 gives every path a magical power, and the one it lists for the Death card is a single word: "Necromancy." Work done with the dug-up dead.

Now Hamlet, act five, the graveyard. A gravedigger is tossing up skulls and singing while he does it, and his song has "a pick-axe, and a spade" in it. Hamlet watches and asks: "Has this fellow no feeling of his business, that he sings at grave-making?" His friend answers: "Custom hath made it in him a property of easiness." The song says the same thing in its next line. Then Hamlet guesses whose skulls they are. "Cain's jaw-bone, that did the first murder." A politician, "one that would circumvent God." "Why may not that be the skull of a lawyer?", getting knocked about "with a dirty shovel." And "a great buyer of land." A killer, a lawyer, a dealer, a man who'd get around God, a shovel, a pick, somebody singing over it, and the word business. One scene.

The record this song is on is called Blood Money. That's the common name for what Judas got paid, and Matthew says what it bought: "the potter's field, to bury strangers in."

THE MOON

"Bloody moon rising with a plague and a flood / Join the mob, join the mob / It's all over, it's all over, it's all over"

Then the sky: a moon coming up the wrong color, a plague, a flood.

The Moon is a card, and its path is one of only three roads off the ground floor of the Tree. 777's note on that road calls it "the essence of error."

Woyzeck is a German play from the 1830s. In it a red moon rises over a killing, and the line is: "Wie ein blutig Eisen." Like a bloody iron.

777 hands out the ten plagues of Egypt, one to each sphere. The ground floor gets "Water turned to Blood." Blood, plague and water in one entry.

Then an invitation and a verdict. The invitation is as old as Proverbs 1: "Cast in thy lot among us; let us all have one purse." So is the answer: "consent thou not." The verdict has a card, the Ten of Swords: a man face down with ten blades in his back. 777's title for it is "Ruin." The card it prints beside it is the Ten of Disks: "Wealth."

THE BAIT

"Goddamn there's always such a big temptation / To be good, to be good / There's always free cheddar in a mousetrap, baby / It's a deal, it's a deal"

The bridge is the pitch. Being decent is the danger, the bait costs nothing, and you shake on it. We're back on the Devil's path, the one from line one.

Waite, on the two chained figures at the Devil's feet: "Hereof is the chain and fatality of the material life." Bait first. Then the bar comes down.

There's a card for the thing he's warning you off. The Six of Pentacles is a merchant with a pair of scales, handing coins to beggars. It sits on the heart's sphere.

And here's the rest of Proverbs 7. The woman who said the master was away with the money finishes her pitch, and the young man follows her "as an ox goeth to the slaughter," "as a bird hasteth to the snare."

In the Bible, the chorus of this song is bait. It's what the one holding the trap says.

THE EYE

"I narrow my eyes like a coin slot baby"

The "I" comes back for the first time since line one. He squints until his eyes are a slot for coins.

The Devil's Hebrew letter is Ayin. Ayin means "eye." The power 777 lists for the Devil's path: "The Witches' Sabbath so-called, the Evil Eye."

The song opened by walking the Devil's path, heart into business. It closes by naming that path's letter. Same road, first line and last picture.

Back to the Sermon on the Mount, two verses after the heart and the treasure: "if thine eye be evil, thy whole body shall be full of darkness." And the verse after that: "Ye cannot serve God and mammon." Mammon is money. Thieves in verse 19. The heart in 21. The eye in 23. God or money in 24. Six verses hold this song's first line, its thieves, its last picture and its title.

THE PHONE

"Let her ring, let her ring"

Then one order: leave her ringing. He doesn't say what "her" is. A phone, a till, a bell.

Judgement is the card of the Call. Waite says its summons is "heard and answered from within." Its path runs from the ground up to Hod. Leave the call ringing and you take that path down.

"Her" has a name in Proverbs 1. "Wisdom crieth without; she uttereth her voice in the streets." Then: "Because I have called, and ye refused; I have stretched out my hand, and no man regarded." Then: "I also will laugh at your calamity." Then: "they shall seek me early, but they shall not find me."

She called. They let it ring. She laughed. Then nobody could find her. That's the hand from verse one, the laugh at the end of the second chorus, this line, and the title. In order.

On the Tree, Wisdom is a sphere: Chokmah, at the top of the Pillar of Mercy. That makes three times the song sends the right-hand side away: the luck, the being good, and her call.

One more thing the song doesn't have. Water, fire and earth are all in it. Air isn't. No wind, no wings, no breath. The only breath is that laugh. 777's note for the letter of Air begins "The Holy Ghost."

THE SLEEPER

Here's where the alibi comes apart.

The Bible has two more ships in trouble. On one, Jonah has run "from the presence of the LORD" and "paid the fare thereof." The storm hits, and he "was gone down into the sides of the ship; and he lay, and was fast asleep." On the other, the disciples are going under, and the one they need is "in the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow." Both times he's on board. Below. Asleep.

Now go back to the pronouns. He says "you" in the first four lines and never again. All that's left of that person is a pet name. The "you" with the heart gets priced, gets left in the ditch, and drops out of the song. Eight lines later the song gives you its title.

The one who went missing is the one he put a price on.

THE WAY UP

Read the map from the bottom. Every climb on the Tree starts on the ground and leaves by one of three doors. The Moon's door is the one 777 calls error. Judgement's door is the Call. Pick up, and that path goes up to Hod. From Hod the Devil's path goes up to the heart. The song walked both of them downhill.

Two ways to hear the end. It stays down, and the last chorus is the seller's last word over a ringing bell. Or it's the same chorus with the phone going behind it, and now you can hear that it's an alibi.

She's still ringing. Who's she calling?
