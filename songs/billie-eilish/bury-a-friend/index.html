🔮 ANTICHRIST DECODE #28: "Bury a Friend" by Billie Eilish

"Bury a Friend" plays the Judgement card in reverse.

The call comes from below.

Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.

Thelema is Greek for "will." It doesn't mean appetite and it doesn't mean permission. It means the one direction a person is built to travel.

The song opens with her name, whispered: "Billie..."

Then: "When we all fall asleep, where do we go?"

Then: "Come here."

A name, a question, a summons. That is the shape of a calling.

The Moon card shows a path rising out of water between two towers, with a dog and a wolf howling on either side.

Waite's guide to the deck calls that path "the issue into the unknown," and the two animals "the fears of the natural mind."

So where do we go when we fall asleep? Onto that path, past those fears.

The monster under the bed is the wolf.

Then: "Like I wanna drown, like I wanna end me."

Read as a symbol, that's the Hanged Man: tied by one foot, upside down, calm, a halo around his head.

Crowley's 777 lists that card as the Man drowned in the "womb" flood. The drowning ends in a birth.

What ends is the self, not the body. The song isn't advocating harm. It uses the oldest picture of ego death there is.

Then: "Bury a friend, try to wake up."

On Judgement an angel calls and the buried rise.

The friend she buries is the old self. But she only tries to wake.

Near the end: "Then my limbs all froze and my eyes won't close."

She's awake and can't rise. Called, but she hasn't answered yet.

On the card the trumpet sounds from the sky. In the song the voice comes from under the bed.

Waite says the summons on that card is "heard and answered from within."

Above, below, within: it's the same voice.

Nothing here claims Eilish built this on purpose. The cards gave these pictures their meanings long before the song existed.

What are you burying, and what wakes up when you do?

Love is the law, love under will.
