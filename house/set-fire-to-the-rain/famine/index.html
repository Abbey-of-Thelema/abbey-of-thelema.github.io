4. KIND

Who speaks: one voice, the singer ("I"), to one "you". Per Adele's own words quoted on Genius (Q&A section), the "you" is a real man: "I was really, really heartbroken when I met who the song's about, and he kind of brought me back to life, and put me back together and… he was a dickhead as well, anyway…". So on the face of it this is a song to a lover, not to a higher self. HIS LENS is a pair of glasses, not a law, and I say where it does not fit: the plain reading is a lover. I note below (section 5d) the few places a higher-self reading is possible; that reading is mine and I do not rest anything on it.

First line to last: the singer lets her heart fall; the "you" rises and claims it and, in her words, saves her from being "over" (L1–4). She is weak-kneed in his arms (L5–8). Then the turn: there was a side of him she never knew, he lied, he always won the games (L9–14). She sets fire to the rain and watches it pour; it burns while she cries; she hears it screaming his name (L15–19). In the second verse she wants to stay lying with him forever, "you and me together" (L20–24), and the pre-chorus and chorus come round again (L25–40), now with "I threw us into the flames… something died… the last time". The third verse is the only moment away from the chorus: she wakes by the door, his caught heart is waiting for him, they are over and she still looks for him (L41–44). The last chorus repeats, and the song ends not with resolution but with "Let it burn" three times (L55–59).

Stories and rumours (where I read them): Genius "About": the title is an allusion to James Taylor's "Fire and Rain"; "Set Fire To The Rain" is about a relationship ending. Wikipedia: the song was written by Adele and Fraser T. Smith; key D minor, 108 bpm; "Though no official music video was released… a live performance from the DVD Live at the Royal Albert Hall was uploaded to… YouTube"; that live version won the Grammy for Best Pop Solo Performance (55th Annual Grammys); and "No proper music video was shot for 'Set Fire to the Rain'… as Adele had recently undergone vocal surgery." Wikipedia, citing a Guardian piece, says Adele took inspiration "when mah lightah stopped workin'" in the wet (I did not open the Guardian piece; "secondhand, via Wikipedia").

Video — what I watched and how: the box cannot play video, so for sound the film is "unwatched" as a playing film; what I read is frames: I downloaded the official upload (1280×720, 3:58) with yt-dlp, cut it at 1 frame per 3 seconds (79 stills; frame N is at (N−1)×3 s), built five contact grids, and opened eleven stills full size. I read the film's timing from its own caption file. Whose account: my own reading of the stills; the video's description lists only shop links.

An honest note on the video lens. The requested lens is the waking world and the climb of the Tree through four worlds, doors and thresholds, and fire against rain. This film does not fit that lens well. It is one continuous live concert in a hall: no story, no doors, no rain, no climb. What it does hold, plainly: a dark concert hall that fills with stage light; the audience; the band and strings; a stage set of rows of lampshades lit from behind (these are lights, not flames); balcony columns seen from above once; small green exit lights in the dark hall; and one blinding stage lamp at 0:39. No door, no fire, no rain and no water is on screen at any point. d. The lens. What the song has, and what it lacks:

  • The call: "I heard it screaming out your name" (L18) is a name being called. Judgement's summons is "heard and answered from within" (Waite:1987); that is the only place the lens's "call" shows.
  • The Moon: "It was dark" (L3) and the whole pre-chorus of deception (L9–14); the gate.
  • "You" as a higher self: does not fit on the plain reading (Adele names a real man). It fits only if "a side to you that I never knew" is read as the unknown part of the higher self. That is mine; nothing rests on it.
  • Breath: a kiss (L4), the scream (L18).
  • Religious words: "saved me" (L4) only. No angels, wings, Saturn, silence, or the World.
  • Blood, drowning, self-harm: "I felt something die" / "something died" (L38, L52) is read as symbol first: the death of the old love or ego, as with Death/Nun and the Hanged Man. It is not cut.

7. PROPOSED A-HAs (he rules)

  1. L15–17, "I set fire to the rain… it burned while I cried" — Thoth, Art: "The fire burns up the water; the water extinguishes the fire." (Thoth:3502). The title is this card's picture; the one difference (Art mingles them harmoniously, the song only destroys) is the song's tragedy.
  2. L15 and L38 — Thoth, Death (Nun): the Scorpion "ringed with fire" and "potassium thrown upon water becomes ignited" (Thoth:3416–21); in Thoth's chemistry, the rain on fire is water ignited, and it is the road (path 24) leaving the gate.
  3. L41–42 "wake up by the door… that heart… must be waiting" — Thomas S.75: "Many are waiting at the door, but those who are one will enter the bridal chamber." (line 374).
  4. L3–4 "It was dark and I was over / Until you kissed my lips and you saved me" — Thoth, Moon: "the poisoned darkness which is the condition of the rebirth of light" and the card's sign, "the Gateway of Resurrection" (Thoth:3934–35, 3917). This is the gate (29), and it comes with 777's "Legs and Feet" (777:2239) for the knees and feet of L6–8.

(A fifth is arguable: Thomas S.10, "I've cast fire on the world, and look, I'm watching over it until it blazes", against "I set fire… watched it pour" and "Let it burn". I graded it MATCH because the song's fire is a heartbreak, not a cosmos; he may rule it up.)

  1. L1 "my heart" and L15 "rain" — Three of Swords: "Three swords piercing a heart; cloud and rain behind." (Waite:2705). A heart and rain, but the heart in the song falls rather than being pierced, and the rain only comes in the chorus. UNVERIFIED as a picture.
  2. L16 "Watched it pour" — Temperance: "It is held to be pouring the essences of life from chalice to chalice." (Waite:1818–19). A shared word and a water picture; the song has no chalice.
  3. L15 — 777: "Fire of Water. … Rain, Springs, etc.—swift passionate attack." (777:2410). Fire and rain in one 777 entry, but the entry is a list of sub-elements.
  4. L4, L16 — AL II:62: "the kisses of the stars rain hard upon thy body." (library line 614). A shared word (kiss, rain) and an ecstatic tone against the song's grief.

  6. L9–10 — Thomas S.91: "you don't know the one right in front of you" (line 425). Not knowing the one in front of you; thin.
  7. L11–12 — 777 Col. L, Hod's virtue "Truthfulness" (777:1260): contrast with "never true", and Hod is not the gate I picked.
  8. L41 — Daleth, "a Door" (777:463, 777:3401; Daleth is path 14, off the climb).

  10. L56 — Shin: "a glyph of the triple tongue of flame" (777:3459–60), for "flames" and "burn"; only the element.

9. DROPPED, AND WHY

  • Numbers in the lyric: none sung; nothing to look up.
  • Any claim that the "you" is the singer's higher self: the singer names a real man; kept only as a note in 5d.
  • The World (path 32) as a gate: the song has no dancer, no wreath; "falling to your feet" fits Earth only by a shared word (Tau as Earth, 777:3476). Dropped.
  • Anything that rested on "Even that" (A) or on Genius's omitted third verse: not used.
  • Thomas 28 (drunk), Thomas 61 (two on a couch; one dies, one lives): "Two will rest on a couch. One will die, the other will live." The couch is "When I lay with you" and "something died" later, but in Thomas it is a person who dies and a different one who lives; too far apart; dropped.
  • Thomas 13 (fire from stones), 16 (fire, sword, war — division): the division is of households, not lovers; dropped.
  • The Lovers (path 17), Two of Cups, Ten of Wands ("oppression", Waite:2260): I read Ten of Wands and found nothing the song says. Dropped.
  • A "James Taylor, Fire and Rain" echo: a title allusion per Genius, not a Tree find.
  • The video's 4-world "climb": not on screen; I will not invent one.
  • Five of Cups and Ace of Cups: not read for this sheet, so no claim either way.
