  • Video unwatched; cover unseen. Steinman's "Vampires in Love" backstory is noted and kept off the line (WEAK or dropped).

  • Death alone brings Joel 2, Revelation 6:12, Amos 8:9–10 and Luke 23:44–45.
  • War and Death alone propose Waite's Judgement-child, "whose back is turned", as an A-HA.
  • Conquest alone puts 777's "squat turrets, blind as the fool's heart" (Qoph note) on the eclipse A-HA, and reads path 24 (Death) as the road up to the heart that the song cannot take.
  • Famine alone contrasts Liber AL II:9 ("all the sorrows are but as shadows; they pass & are done") with "a shadow on me all of the time", and notes Thoth's "The moon has no air" against the song's missing Air.

KIND

Agreed, all four. Power ballad, 1983, written and produced by Jim Steinman. Two voices. Bonnie Tyler sings the verses and chorus to a "you", named in verse 3 as "the boy". Rory Dodd sings the "(Turn around)" and "Turn around, bright eyes" call (Wikipedia, "Background and composition"; Death: "The duet with Rory Dodd"). HIS LENS warning applies: reading the two as one voice, or the "you" as only a higher self, wrecks the song. Verse 3's "no one in the universe / as magical and wondrous as you" is where the lens might fit; Famine, Death and War mark that as their reading only.

What happens, agreed: "every now and then" she is lonely, tired, nervous, terrified; she sees "the look in your eyes" and "fall[s] apart". The bridge begs to be held "tonight"; "Forever's gonna start tonight". Then the turn: "Once upon a time I was falling in love / But now I'm only falling apart", and "Once upon a time there was light in my life / But now there's only love in the dark / … A total eclipse of the heart". It ends on the call, unanswered.

Stories around it, from Wikipedia's citations of Playbill and Record Mirror, not opened at the source (all four name this): Steinman, "I actually wrote that to be a vampire love song. Its original title was 'Vampires in Love'… It's all about the darkness, the power of darkness and love's place in the dark." Tyler: someone "lying there in complete darkness" / "who wants to love so badly she's lying there in complete darkness." "Turn around, bright eyes" comes from Steinman's 1969 musical The Dream Engine. Meat Loaf said it was written for him; Tyler and Steinman denied it (Conquest, from Wikipedia).

Video: unwatched, all four. Account used: Wikipedia (Russell Mulcahy, Holloway Sanatorium; Tyler in white; boarding-school boys; a choir). Death also cites lyrics.com for glowing eyes, ninjas and doves, still unwatched. Cover: not seen.

THE TREE
