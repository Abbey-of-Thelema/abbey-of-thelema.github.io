What Thelema is

The Book of the Law was dictated in Cairo between noon and 1 p.m. on three days, 8, 9 and 10 April 1904. Crowley wrote it down; he said the author called himself Aiwass. The book lays down a short code: “Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.” “Love is the law, love under will.” “There is no law beyond Do what thou wilt.” “The word of the Law is Θελημα”, Thelema, which is the Greek for Will. Crowley adds that it has the same number as Agape, the Greek for Love. The book also says: “Every man and every woman is a star.”

Source: Liber AL vel Legis (The Book of the Law), with Crowley’s Introduction (Introduction, parts I and III; chapter I, verses 3, 39, 40 and 57). Copy on file: the O.T.O. U.S. Grand Lodge library text.

Who Aleister Crowley was

Aleister Crowley was born Edward Alexander Crowley on 12 October 1875 in Leamington Spa, England, and died on 1 December 1947 in Hastings. He studied at Trinity College, Cambridge. He was an occultist, ceremonial magician, poet, novelist, mountaineer and painter, and he founded Thelema. 777, his book of correspondences, was first published in London in 1909; this site reads the Tree from its 1955 reprint, 777 Revised.

Sources: the Wikipedia article “Aleister Crowley” (copy on file); the title page of 777 Revised.

What the Tree is

Dion Fortune: the Tree of Life is “the Ten Holy Sephiroth arranged in a particular pattern”, joined by lines called the Thirty-two Paths. Count them and there are twenty-two; the first ten “paths” are the ten spheres themselves. So the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet go on the twenty-two paths, and with them the twenty-two tarot trumps. 777 puts it the same way: “32 paths, of 10 numbers and 22 letters”.

Sources: Dion Fortune, The Mystical Qabalah (1935), chapter IV; 777 Revised, “On the Magical Alphabet”, p. 132.

How the climb works

Fortune writes that force comes down the Tree as a Lightning Flash and ends in Malkuth, and that the way back up is shown as the Serpent of Wisdom, “whose coils loop upwards on the Paths till its head rests beside Kether”; the serpent “is the symbol of initiation”. On this site you start at Malkuth, the Kingdom, and climb by the doors: path 32 to Yesod, path 31 to Hod, path 29 to Netzach, and on up.

Sources: Dion Fortune, The Mystical Qabalah, chapter XXV, §22; the path numbers are 777’s (Col. XII and the house’s Tree table).
