In Fight Club, Tyler Durden and Marla Singer embody primal forces in a Gnostic-Thelemic ascent up the Tree of Life—from Malkuth’s chains through Yesod’s dreams to Tiphareth’s unity—mirroring the Great Work: shattering illusions to realize True Will. Tyler is the demon, qliphothic fury that tears veils; Marla the angel, smoke-veiled Shekinah who stabilizes chaos.

Gnostic core: Tyler defies the archons, blasting the Demiurge’s prison and igniting gnosis against hylic traps like jobs and possessions. Marla redeems pneumatically, exposing fakery in support groups and guiding from slumber to liberation.

Thelemic dynamic: Tyler’s chaotic Will unleashes the ordeal; Marla grounds it into sovereignty, their polarity forging the star.

Malkuth: The Narrator trapped in catalog tombs and insomnia. Tyler burns the condo, turns fat into soap—“earth from fire”—and dissolves inertia. Marla offers the lifeline.

Tiphareth: The path of Samekh (Temperance/Art)—the angel blends water and fire over the cauldron, uniting opposites. Echoed in Tyler’s soap business card: two figures sharing one crown. The split personality becomes sacred polarity. Self-inflicted wound banishes Tyler; collapsing towers perform Geburah’s pruning; intellect and passion fuse. Marla holds the center—True Will dawns.

Summon demon Tyler to disrupt, embrace angel Marla to redeem; climb the thorns to godhood. Book and film are the blueprint: hit bottom, unite opposites, emerge as the star-self.
