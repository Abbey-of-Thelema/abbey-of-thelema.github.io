THE DECODE, AS POSTED

Ever Notice these Trilogies Ends With a Child???

Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.

I finished the third Batman breakdown this week and then noticed the Matrix does it too. And Lord of the Rings.

Hold that. I have to show you three films first.

The first one burns his house down.

Not a metaphor. Wayne Manor goes up while he is standing in it, and at the end he says he will rebuild it. Brick for brick. The one thing he inherits that he actually restores.

Then watch the next two films. He never lives in it.

He rebuilds a house and moves into the basement. Eight years later the upstairs is a locked wing with sheets over the furniture. He restored the shell of his father's life and buried himself under it.

There is a book with three chapters that runs the same way. The first one is a field. Space, drawn wide before anybody moves in it. A house with nobody in it yet. Keep that in the corner of your eye.

The second film ends at a door.

Everyone remembers the speech and the manhunt. Almost nobody remembers what the camera does last — it leaves a commissioner standing at a post he has decided to keep, and sends the other man out into the dark.

Two men at one door. One stays. One goes.

The film does not tell you that is a question. It plays like an ending. Chapter two of that book is a point. One thing, alone, moving in the dark.

The third film pays a bill from a kitchen.

Early on, before anything has gone wrong, Alfred describes a fantasy out loud. Florence. A café on the river. He would look up and see the boy at a table across the room with a life around him, and they would not need to speak.

He says it as a regret. It is not a regret. It is the ending, delivered in act one, at full volume, while everybody including the audience is thinking about something else.

When the film finally pays it, there is no dialogue. There does not need to be. It was already said.

Now put all three side by side.

Film one he acquires. A name, a company, a mission, a house. Film two he spends. The name goes to a lie, the mission goes to a manhunt. Film three it is taken. All of it. In one morning.

Buy, spend, seized. One transaction across seven hours, and the man in the last frame owns nothing. No house. No company. No mask.

That is the only state the last thing can happen from.

Because the door from film two opens again. The man who kept the post takes the mask off and leaves it on a shelf. Somebody else walks through. A young cop who was never the story. He gives a clerk his legal name and a cave lights up under a house that was never his.

Here is where it stopped being about Batman.

The little girl talking to the Oracle on the bench at the end of the last Matrix film. She made the sunrise. She is not the hero of anything. She is what the entire war was for, and the final frame belongs to her.

Sam's daughter in the doorway at the end of the last Lord of the Rings film. Those books were never about Sam. He carried the one who carried it. The last shot of the trilogy is his door, his house, his kid.

Three trilogies. Three last frames. Not one of them belongs to the person you watched for nine hours.

Chapter three of that book is a child. Crowned.

He does not get to watch the boy grow up. That is what the ending is. Not sad. Just not his anymore.
