DECODE #32 · Linkin Park

“In the End”

Posted Nov 9, 2026. Posted only on X.

READ THE LIVE POST ON XX ARTICLE PAGE

THE DECODE, AS POSTED

In the End shipped to American radio on September 11, 2001. Its chorus is the Tower card. If you think that’s a code, you’re half right.
Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.
(Thelema is Greek for “will”: not what you want this minute, but what you’d still want in the end.)
I’m not telling you what Linkin Park meant. I’m laying the old maps over this song, one line at a time, and showing you what lines up. Keep a tarot deck open in another tab and look at every card I name.
The key: two men sing this song, and heard this way, they’re one man. Mike Shinoda raps the verses: the mind, working it out in words. Chester Bennington sings the chorus and the bridge: the heart, feeling it. When either of them says “you,” hear the higher self, the one Crowley called the Holy Guardian Angel.
THE ONE
“It starts with one / One thing, I don’t know why”
The first line ends on “one,” and the next line starts on it. The word gets handed across.
Kether (Hebrew, “crown”) is the top of the Tree of Life, the old map of ten spheres that shows how the One comes down into the world and how a soul climbs back up. Everything starts at Kether. So does this song.
The Emerald Tablet, alchemy’s founding text, in Isaac Newton’s own translation: “the miracles of one only thing,” and “all things have their birth from this one thing.”
In the Gospel of Thomas (sayings of Jesus dug up in Egypt in 1945), the disciples ask how their end will come. Jesus asks whether they’ve found the beginning yet: “For where the beginning is, there will the end be.” The song is called In the End, and the first word it leans on is “one.”
And not knowing why? A medieval English mystic wrote a whole book, The Cloud of Unknowing, about reaching God by not knowing. That one’s a maybe.
THE ANSWER
“It doesn’t even matter how hard you try”
The Book of the Law (the book Aleister Crowley said was dictated to him in Cairo in 1904), chapter one, verse 44: “For pure will, unassuaged of purpose, delivered from the lust of result, is every way perfect.” Straining for a result is the trap. Will isn’t effort.
He hands you the answer in the third line, before the struggle starts. The chorus only repeats it. That’s Thomas again: the end was in the beginning.
And there’s “matter.” Malkuth (Hebrew, “kingdom”), the bottom sphere of the Tree, is the sphere of matter. Keep that word. It’s the last one you’ll hear.
THE RHYME
“Keep that in mind, I designed this rhyme to explain in due time”
Hod (Hebrew, “splendor”) is the sphere of Mercury, the god of words. A man designing a rhyme is talking from Hod. Look at the Magician too, Mercury’s card, laying out his tools to plan the work. That one’s thinner.
“In due time” is a Bible phrase. 1 Peter 5:6: “Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time.” Brought low first, raised after. That’s this song: the fall, then the rise.
He builds the rhyme now, and the meaning comes later. The lesson waits until the student is ready.
THE PENDULUM
“All I know, time is a valuable thing, watch it fly by as the pendulum swings”
The Kybalion (a 1908 book of Hermetic teaching) calls it the Principle of Rhythm: “Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall; the pendulum-swing manifests in everything; the measure of the swing to the right is the measure of the swing to the left; rhythm compensates.” Rise and fall. That’s the chorus.
Look at the World card. On the Tree, its path is Tav (ת), path 32, the door out of Malkuth up to Yesod. Crowley’s book of correspondences, 777, gives it Saturn, the lord of time.
Saturn is Father Time, and painters give him an hourglass and wings. Time that flies is Saturn with wings.
Waite’s meanings for the World even include “voyage” and “flight.” That one’s a pun.
And a pendulum swings side to side, between the two outer pillars of the Tree. The climb goes straight up the middle, where the swing can’t reach.
THE NIGHT OF TIME
“Watch it count down to the end of the day, the clock ticks life away”
The Golden Dawn (the Victorian magical order that both big modern tarot decks came out of) gave the World card a secret title: “The Great One of the Night of Time.” This line counts the day down into night. The night of time.
In the myth, Saturn eats his own children. The clock ticking life away is the old god of time, eating.
And the end of the day is the sun going down. On the Tree, the sun is Tiphareth (Hebrew, “beauty”), the heart of the Tree, the god who dies and rises. Watch for that sunset again in verse two.
THE EDGE
“It’s so unreal, didn’t look out below, watch the time go right out the window”
Look at the Fool, stepping off a cliff with his eyes on the sky. Arthur Waite, who designed the deck the artist Pamela Colman Smith painted, wrote that the Fool “surveys the blue distance before him—its expanse of sky rather than the prospect below.” And: “The edge which opens on the depth has no terror.” Not looking below is the Fool’s whole picture. Every journey starts with that step.
Now look at the Moon. Waite’s meanings for it include “deception” and “error.” The unreal world in this line is the Moon’s world of illusion.
The time going out the window: the moment slipping out of the house, out of the body. A stretch.
And Waite says the sun behind the Fool “knows whence he came, whither he is going, and how he will return by another path after many days.” Remember that return.
THE GRIP
“Tryna hold on, d-didn’t even know, I wasted it all just to watch you go”
The Four of Pentacles: a man clutching his coins. Waite: “The surety of possessions, cleaving to that which one has.” Hanging on.
Then “you” goes. Hear “you” as the higher self, and he wasted everything watching it leave. That’s the fall: a soul losing sight of its angel.
He lost it before he even understood what it was.
THE STOREHOUSE
“I kept everything inside, and even though I tried, it all fell apart”
The Ten of Swords: a man face down with ten swords in his back. Crowley titled it “Ruin.” The Tens sit at Malkuth, and Swords are the mind. The mind’s verse ends in Ruin.
Crowley’s Art card has an old alchemists’ motto written across it, V.I.T.R.I.O.L.: visit the interior of the earth, and by rectifying you will find the hidden stone. What he buried inside is where the stone is. Possible.
Thomas, saying 3: “the kingdom is inside of you, and it is outside of you.”
And everything kept inside is Yesod (Hebrew, “foundation”), the sphere just above Malkuth: the storehouse.
“What it meant to me will eventually be a memory of a time when”
Yesod is the Moon’s sphere, the sphere of memory. Dion Fortune, one of the great teachers of the Tree, calls it “the Treasure House of Images, the sphere of Maya, Illusion.” There’s the unreal world from the edge.
Now listen to how this line ends. It doesn’t. Mike stops mid-thought, and Chester’s chorus finishes the sentence. One sentence, two voices. One speaker.
THE TOWER
“I tried so hard, and got so far”
Here’s the heart. Netzach (Hebrew, “victory”) is the sphere of Venus: feeling. 777 gives its vision as “Beauty Triumphant.”
Look at the Tower. Genesis 11:4: “let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven.” Trying hard and getting far is building toward heaven by effort.
And the pendulum: how far it swung up is how far it comes down.
“But in the end, it doesn’t even matter”
The Book of the Law, verse 44 again, “delivered from the lust of result,” and this time the heart is saying it.
This is the end the disciples asked about. And “matter” again: Malkuth.
“I had to fall to lose it all”
The Tower is Hebrew Peh (פ), path 27, the planet Mars: lightning, a crown blown off, two people falling.
Here’s the big one. On the Tree, the Tower’s path runs across between Hod and Netzach: Mike’s sphere and Chester’s. It crosses the arrow path that climbs from Yesod to Tiphareth, the heart of the Tree. You can’t reach the heart until the Tower comes down. The fall isn’t the failure. It’s the gate.
Matthew 16:25: “whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall find it.”
The Easter Vigil hymn sings “O happy fault”: felix culpa, the fortunate fall. The fall had to happen for the rising to happen.
777 gives Tiphareth’s power as “The Vision of the Harmony of Things (also the Mysteries of the Crucifixion).” Losing it all is the crucifixion half. Then the chorus lands on “matter” one more time.
THE DATE
Back to the date. In the End shipped to American radio on September 11, 2001, and you’ve just looked at the Tower card.
Nearly three thousand people died that day. That isn’t a code, and nobody here is making a joke of it.
Here’s the other half. Labels set radio dates weeks ahead, and the song had already been out on the album since October 2000. No one at a record label knew what that Tuesday would bring.
Waite’s meaning for the Tower: “It is a card in particular of unforeseen catastrophe.” Unforeseen. The card itself tells you nobody saw it coming.
The Tower card is over five hundred years old. It keeps turning up when towers fall because that’s what it was drawn for: the moment something built too high comes down, in a city or in one man’s life. And Babel, the tower from the chorus, is in Genesis chapter 11. Coincidences like that are real, and this whole map is made of them. They point at you, not at a conspiracy.
“Occult” just means hidden. What’s hidden here is a very old map of what happens to people, drawn so well it keeps matching the news. The symbol was never the secret. The secret is that it’s about you.
ROUND TWO
“One thing, I don’t know why / It doesn’t even matter how hard you try”
Verse two starts on the one thing again. The circle has turned once, and he gives you the answer again before the second struggle.
THE MIX
“Keep that in mind, I designed this rhyme to remind myself how I tried so hard”
The rhyme turns around. In verse one he built it to explain to you. Now he builds it to remind himself. The audience has become himself.
And listen to how it ends: Chester’s chorus words, trying so hard, coming out of Mike. The rap is speaking the heart’s lines. The voices are mixing.
Look at Art (Temperance in most decks), Hebrew Samekh (ס), path 25. In Waite’s deck it’s an angel pouring between two cups, making them one. It’s happening inside the song.
THE MOCKING
“In spite of the way you were mockin’ me, actin’ like I was part of your property”
Look at the Devil: Hebrew Ayin (ע), path 26, the sign of Capricorn, and Capricorn is ruled by Saturn, the same clock from verse one. Two figures stand chained at his feet. Waite: “Hereof is the chain and fatality of the material life.” Owned like property.
Matthew 27:29: the soldiers “mocked him, saying, Hail, King of the Jews!” Verse two opens the Passion, and the mocking comes first.
THE WAR
“Rememberin’ all the times you fought with me”
The Five of Swords, which Crowley titled “Defeat”: a man collecting swords while two beaten figures walk away. A weaker one.
It’s the two halves at war with each other.
And the Lovers card belongs to Gemini, the twins. The Golden Dawn’s title for it is “The Children of the Voice.” Two voices born of one. A stretch.
“I’m surprised it got so far”
Chester’s “so far,” in Mike’s verse. The mix again. The climb went further than the climber thought it could.
“Things aren’t the way they were before”
Death, card 13, Hebrew Nun (נ), path 24: change past the point of no return. Not a body dying. The old self ending. A weaker one.
UNRECOGNIZED
“You wouldn’t even recognize me anymore”
Luke 24:16: on the road to Emmaus, the risen Jesus walks beside two of his own, “But their eyes were holden that they should not know him.” John 20:14: Mary “saw Jesus standing, and knew not that it was Jesus.” After the rising, his own people don’t know him.
Count it up: mocked, lost everything, risen and unrecognized. Verse two is the Passion, all the way through Easter.
And that’s Tiphareth, the sphere of the god who dies and rises. Crowley called what happens there the Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel: meeting your higher self face to face.
BACK
“Not that you knew me back then, but it all comes back to me in the end”
Thomas, saying 3, goes on: “When you come to know yourselves, then you will become known… But if you will not know yourselves, you dwell in poverty.” The other half never knew him, because he didn’t know himself.
Here’s another big one. Mike’s verse lands on “in the end.” Chester’s chorus lands on “in the end.” Two voices, the same three words: the angel and the devil on your shoulders, saying the same thing to the one man between them.
The Golden Dawn has a saying: “Kether is in Malkuth, and Malkuth is in Kether.” You end where you began. That’s the Fool’s sun, which knew “how he will return by another path.”
THE FLIP
“You kept everything inside, and even though I tried, it all fell apart”
The same line as verse one, with one word changed. Then it was “I” who kept everything inside. Now it’s “you.” The I and the you trade places. The singer and the one he sings to are the same person.
Thomas, saying 22: “When you make the two one, and when you make the inside like the outside…”
“What it meant to me will eventually be a memory of a time when”
And the sentence gets handed across again. Two voices, one sentence.
The chorus comes back word for word, but after the Passion it lands differently. The fall reads as the crucifixion now, and the verdict, that none of it matters, is the risen one, calm.
THE STAR
“I’ve put my trust in you”
Look at the Star, the card right after the Tower. Waite’s meanings for it: “Loss, theft, privation, abandonment; another reading says—hope and bright prospects.” Losing everything and trusting anyway, on one card.
Hear “you” as the higher self. The heart has put its trust in its angel. After the Tower, the Star.
THE ARROW
“Pushed as far as I can go”
Art again. Samekh’s sign is Sagittarius, the archer, and Samekh’s arrow flies straight up the middle of the Tree, from Yesod to Tiphareth. Pushed as far as it can go: that’s the arrow at the top of its flight.
And it’s the Fool’s cliff again. As far as you can go is the edge.
THE ONE THING
“For all this, there’s only one thing you should know”
He says there’s one thing you should know. Then he never says what it is. The song names the One and keeps it.
The Book of the Law, chapter one, verse 22: Nuit, the goddess of the night sky, is known to him “by a secret name which I will give him when at last he knoweth me.”
The Tao Te Ching opens the same way: the Way you can put into words isn’t the eternal Way. Crowley did his own version of it, Liber 157. Possible.
And “one thing” sends you back to the first line, and to the Emerald Tablet’s “one thing.”
The bridge goes around twice, like the two voices. A stretch, but count it.
THE LAST WORD
“But in the end, it doesn’t even matter”
The first word the song leans on is “one.” Its last word is “matter.” On the Tree, Kether down to Malkuth is the Lightning Flash, the zigzag the One takes down into the world. The song is that descent, and the climb runs back up it.
Matter is Malkuth. The song ends back on the floor of the Tree, where it started, knowing what it didn’t know before.
THE VOICES
Four ways to hear that they’re one man:
They finish each other’s sentences, twice.
Verse two speaks the chorus’s words: trying hard, getting far, the end.
The flip: “I” kept everything inside, then “you” did.
Both of them land on “in the end.”
An angel on one shoulder and a devil on the other, saying the same thing. The man between them stands on the Middle Pillar, the straight road up the Tree.
FIRE AND WATER
There’s no fire in this song and no water. Not one word of either. There’s air: flying, a window. There’s earth: below, falling, property, matter. Listen to who’s singing. That’s where the fire and water went.
The Golden Dawn climbed the Tree in grades, and each of the first four grades has an element. Hod’s grade is Water. Netzach’s is Fire. Mike raps from Hod, so he’s the water. Chester sings from Netzach, so he’s the fire. Art is the card that mixes the two (Crowley’s Art pours fire and water into one cauldron), and its path runs straight up between them. The lyric never makes the mix. The two voices do. The album is called Hybrid Theory.
And the two elements the words do have are the first two grades: Earth for Malkuth, Air for Yesod. The words climb the first two rungs, and the voices carry the next two. All four elements, all four grades. Past them, through a Portal, the next grade belongs to Tiphareth, and the Golden Dawn’s ceremony for it has a Cross of Suffering and the tomb of the order’s founder. That’s verse two.
THE VIDEO
Nathan “Karma” Cox and the band’s Joe Hahn directed it. The band plays on top of a giant statue that stands like a tower over a desert. Near the end, the sky goes dark, rain falls, and the ground turns green. A tower, then water pouring down: the Tower, then the Star. That rain is the only water in the whole thing.
THE CLIMB
Malkuth, inside time → the World’s door, Saturn’s → Yesod, memory and the storehouse → Hod and Netzach, Mike and Chester, with the Tower between them → Samekh, the arrow, pushed as far as it goes → Tiphareth, the Passion: mocked, fallen, unrecognized → Kether, the one thing, named and never said → back to Malkuth on the last word.
Two ways to hear the ending. It reaches the heart in verse two and comes all the way home. Or the chorus keeps swinging it back down, and it’s still on the pendulum.
I could be wrong about any one of these. I can’t explain all of them.
He never tells you the one thing. So what is it?
Love is the law, love under will.
