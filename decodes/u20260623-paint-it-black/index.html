ANTICHRIST DECODE — PAINT IT BLACK
The red door is the setting sun.

The Rolling Stones, 1966 · Atu XX, Judgement · the trumpet of Shin, Path 31, sounding at the summit.

Listen to what the song tells you twice. He wants the sun “blotted out from the sky.” He stares “hard enough into the setting sun.” The red door he keeps trying to paint black is the sun going down in the west. This is not a man sinking into grief. It is a man climbing the Tree of Life at dusk, following the dying sun up through the western gate. He climbs the Middle Pillar at sunset. That is the whole song.

MALKUTH — the black kingdom.

PROFANE: his world has gone black with loss; nothing holds color anymore.

INITIATE: the material kingdom seen as a corpse — the blackened ground the Work begins from. “It’s not easy facing up when your whole world is black.”

PATH 32 (Tav · Saturn) — the red door, the western gate.

PROFANE: he wants the red door painted black, the daylight gone.

INITIATE: the red door is the setting sun; he wills the light down so the western gate will open. The song names its own door — he wants the sun blotted out from the sky. “I see a red door and I want it painted black.”

YESOD — the lunar mirror.

PROFANE: girls pass in summer clothes and he looks away, sealed in his darkness.

INITIATE: “summer” is the sun at full strength, Tiphareth blazing at noon — a glory he is too broken to face. He turns his head not from beauty but from a light he cannot yet bear risen. “I have to turn my head until my darkness goes.”

PATH 25 (Samekh · Sagittarius) — the arrow of meditation.

PROFANE: he stares into the setting sun, desperate for one more glimpse of her.

INITIATE: the eye held fast to the dying disc — the arrow aimed straight at the heart of the Tree. “If I look hard enough into the setting sun.”

TIPHARETH — the heart, black, because the sun has set.

PROFANE: he looks inside and finds his heart black; maybe she’ll laugh with him again by morning.

INITIATE: the sol niger, the black sun of the alchemists — the gold must darken in the grave before it is reborn brighter than it went down. “My love will laugh with me before the morning comes.”

The sitar drone never resolves. The song refuses to climb — and that refusal is the climb. Everyone hears a man begging the world to go dark. What he is actually doing is burying the sun in the west on purpose, so that it has somewhere to rise from. The black heart was never the end of him. It was the seed. He didn’t lose the light. He planted it.

The morning he keeps promising himself never arrives inside the song — because the song ends one breath before the trumpet sounds. He is still in the coffin, waiting. So am I. So is everyone reading this.

The only question left is who’s already heard it start to play.

Love is the law, love under will.
