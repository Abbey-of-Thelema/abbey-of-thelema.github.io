🔮 ANTICHRIST DECODE #27: "Hero" by Chad Kroeger

The Spider-Man song tells you which tarot card to grab.

It's hiding in the corner of the last card in the deck, where you've never looked.

Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.
Thelema is Greek for Will. Not "do whatever you want." The thing you are actually here to do.

"Hero" is a song about refusing a savior, and it names the door he leaves by.

You don't need to know anything about tarot to check this. Just look at the pictures.

The song opens with a man so high he can hear heaven, and heaven doesn't hear him back. There's a card where an angel in the clouds blows a trumpet and the dead climb out of their coffins to answer it. It's called Judgement. In the song the trumpet only goes one way. He hears it. Nobody comes down.

So he stops waiting: "I'll hold on to the wings of the eagles." Pull up the last card in the deck, the World, in either deck, and look at the four corners. A man, a lion, a bull, and an eagle. Now read what A. E. Waite, whose deck is the one you've seen, wrote that card means: voyage, route, emigration, flight, change of place. Next line of the song: "Watch as we all fly away."

You did that yourself. I didn't tell you anything you couldn't see.

Here's the part I spent years on. Those two cards are two of only three doors out of the bottom of the Tree of Life (the map the tarot is laid over). The bottom is Malkuth (Hebrew, "the kingdom"), the ordinary world, where everyone stands around waiting for a hero. In 1907 Crowley wrote a children's story about that map, a little girl walking through a palace, in a book called Konx Om Pax. The bottom floor is the Servants' Hall: "nearly everybody stays there all their lives." The first passage out is guarded by four creatures, and the first one named is "an eagle that flew, and beat his wings." The other passage out is "all fire and flames and full of coffins," with "an Angel blowing ever so hard on a trumpet, and people getting up out of the coffins." Then the line that could be this song's chorus: "Most people never wake up for anything less."

Eliphas Lévi had drawn the same two pictures fifty years earlier: a trumpet, and the dead rising from their tombs; and a crown between "four mysterious animals," man, eagle, lion, bull. He gives each one an element. The eagle is air. Wings.

And the hero? The Book of the Law has one line for that: "Every man and every woman is a star." Nobody is coming. You're the one with the light. Then it says what to do with it: "Put on the wings, and arouse the coiled splendour within you: come unto me!"

The book the phrase "Holy Guardian Angel" comes from, Abramelin, is one long instruction: nobody does this for you. Six months of your own work, morning and evening, and only then: "your Guardian Angel is already about you, though Invisible, and conducteth and governeth your heart." Heaven didn't ignore him. It was waiting for him to move.

And the love that was supposed to save us, "look what love gave us"? Same book as the star, same chapter: "Nor let the fools mistake love; for there are love and love. There is the dove, and there is the serpent. Choose ye well!" Love by itself saves nobody. Love under will is a door.

The pictures were on the cards a hundred years before the song. I'm not smarter than anyone; I just stared at them until I couldn't unsee them.

So which hero were you waiting for: the one who comes down, or the one who grabs the eagle?

Love is the law, love under will.
