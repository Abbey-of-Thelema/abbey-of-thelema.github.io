Tiphareth · Beauty

The sixth sphere, the centre of the Tree (Fortune). Every line below names its book. 777 comes first.

The books: 777 Revised, Aleister Crowley (the 1955 Neptune Press reprint of 777, 1909), in the Celephaïs Press text of 2004; and Dion Fortune, The Mystical Qabalah, first published in London in 1935 (Weiser edition, 1999 printing). § numbers are Fortune’s paragraphs. “Row” is 777’s row: row 6 is Tiphareth; Fortune’s chapter XX is Tiphareth.

What it is

Tiphareth means Beauty. It is the sixth sphere. (777, Cols. II and III, row 6.)

Its heaven is Shemesh, the Sphere of Sol, the Sun. (777, Cols. VI and VII, row 6.)

Fortune: it stands “in the centre of the Pillar of Equilibrium”. (Fortune, ch. XX, the table.)

Fortune gives three keys to it: “it is the centre of equilibrium of the whole Tree”; “it is Kether on a lower arc and Yesod on a higher arc”; and “it is the point of transmutation between the planes of force and the planes of form.” “From the point of view of Kether it is a child; from the point of view of Malkuth it is a king; and from the point of view of the transmutation of force it is a sacrificed god.” (Fortune, ch. XX, §1.)

Its virtue: devotion to the Great Work

777, in its column of Transcendental Morality, gives row 6 the words Devotion to Great Work. (777, Col. L, row 6.)

Fortune: “The characteristic virtue of the Sphere of Tiphareth is Devotion to this Great Work.” “Devotion might be defined as love for something higher than ourselves”. (Fortune, ch. XX, §81.)

Its vice: 777 gives the Sun (row 30) “[Pride]”; Fortune: “The Vice assigned to Tiphareth is Pride”, and “as long as we are self-centred we cannot be made one with all things.” (777, Col. L, row 30; Fortune, ch. XX, §82.)

Its spiritual experience: the Vision of the Harmony of Things

777: “The Vision of the Harmony of Things (also the Mysteries of the Crucifixion)”, with “[[Beatific Vision]]” added in 777 Revised. (777, Col. XLV, row 6.)

Fortune: “two spiritual experiences go to make up the initiation of Tiphareth, the Vision of the Harmony of Things and the Vision of the Mysteries of the Crucifixion.” (Fortune, ch. XX, §77.)

Fortune: in the Vision of the Harmony of Things “we understand that the natural is but the dense aspect of the spiritual”. (Fortune, ch. XX, §78.) The Mysteries of the Crucifixion “concern the magical power of sacrifice”. (Fortune, ch. XX, §80.)

Fortune, on Malkuth: its experience is “the bringing down of the Godhead into manhood, just as the spiritual experience of Tiphareth is the taking up of manhood into the Godhead.” (Fortune, ch. XXV, §89.)

Its God-name and the rest

  • God-name: Jehovah Eloah va-Daath. 777’s note: “The reference is to Tiphereth as the child of Chokmah and Binah, Daäth (their first child) having failed to find a place on the Tree.” (777, Col. V, row 6, and its note.) Fortune spells it Tetragrammaton Aloah Va Daath. (Fortune, the table.)
  • Archangel: Raphael. (777, Col. XCIX, row 6.)
  • Choir of angels: Malakim; Fortune: “Malachim, Kings”. (777, Col. C, row 6; Fortune, the table.)
  • Magical image: “A majestic king, a child, a crucified god.” (777, Col. CXXI, row 6.) Fortune has “a sacrificed god”. (Fortune, the table.)
  • Path of wisdom: the Intelligence of the Mediating Influence. (777, Col. XIII, row 6.) Fortune: the Mediating Intelligence. (Fortune, the table.)
  • Tarot: the four Sixes. (777, Col. XIV, row 6; Fortune, the table.)
  • Colours, in 777’s four scales: King, clear pink rose; Queen, yellow (gold); Emperor (Yetzirah), rich salmon; Empress, gold amber. (777, Cols. XV to XVIII, row 6.) Fortune gives clear rose-pink, yellow, rich salmon-pink and golden amber. (Fortune, the table.)
  • In the body: the breast. (Fortune, the table.)
  • Symbols: the Lamen, the Rosy Cross, the Calvary Cross, the truncated pyramid, the cube. (Fortune, the table.)

What it means for someone climbing

Fortune: “In Tiphareth the initiate takes the first grade of adepthood”. (Fortune, ch. XXIV, §14.)

Fortune: devotion “is a very important factor in the Way of Initiation that leads to the higher consciousness”, and “It is this Devotion, rising to Adoration, in the Great Work, which initiates us into the Mysteries of the Crucifixion.” (Fortune, ch. XX, §81.)
